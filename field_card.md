# SheAlert Audio Recording Field Card (v2)

> Please print this page and bring it to every recording session.

Thank you for helping us build SheAlert — a safety app that listens quietly in the
background and recognises real distress before a person can even reach their phone.
Your voice teaches the app what genuine urgency sounds like in Urdu, so that one day
it can tell the difference between danger and an ordinary conversation. Everything you
say in this session is acting. There is no real danger at any point. You may stop, skip
a line, or leave at any time, for any reason or none, and any recording of yours can be
deleted later just by giving us your volunteer ID.

## How this card is organised

This card has two short parts: one for female volunteers and one for male volunteers.
Both parts use the same three types of recording — **Distress**, **Aggression**, and
**Everyday Talking** — and the same script, so recordings stay easy to compare. Only
the volunteer ID range and the number of clips differ between the two parts.

## Session Targets

| Group | Volunteer IDs | Clips / person | Approx. time | Total clips |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Female volunteers | F01 – F75 (75 people) | 20 | ~10 minutes | 1,500 |
| Male volunteers | M01 – M20 (20 people) | 30 | ~15 minutes | 600 |

**Why the two sides look different.** Earlier drafts asked every volunteer, regardless
of gender, for 30 clips. In practice we expect it will be much easier to find willing
male volunteers than female ones, so we have redesigned the split: more female
volunteers, but a lighter session for each one. The female target has grown from 50 to
75 people while the request per person has dropped from 30 clips to 20 — so the total
number of female clips collected (1,500) has not changed at all. The male side is left
exactly as before. This keeps the dataset the same overall size while making it far
easier and faster for any one female volunteer to say yes.

## Before Every Session (applies to both parts)

1. Sign the consent form with the volunteer. Give them their ID (example: F01 or M01).
   Do not write their name anywhere else.
2. Explain: everything they will say is acting. There is no real danger. They can stop
   at any time without giving a reason.
3. Set the phone to 16 kHz, 16-bit, mono. Hold or place the phone 30 cm from the mouth.
4. Record one 5-second test clip. Check it is clear and not too loud or too quiet.
5. Confirm which environment(s) this volunteer will record in today.

**Say this to the volunteer:** "We will record a few short sections together. All of it
is acting, and you're welcome to take a breath any time you need to."

## Part 1 — Female Volunteers (IDs F01 – F75)

This part is for female volunteers only. Give each one an ID in this range, for
example F01, F02, and so on. Each volunteer records all three sections below — 20
clips in total, about 10 minutes of recording.

### Section 1: Distress — 8 clips

Say each word twice: once mild, once urgent. Let the second time sound noticeably more
urgent than the first.

| Word (say it) | Meaning | Take 1 (Mild) | Take 2 (Urgent) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bachao | Save me / Help | Calm, slightly worried | Louder, clearly urgent |
| Madad karo | Help me | Calm, slightly worried | Louder, clearly urgent |
| Choro mujhe | Let go of me | Calm, slightly worried | Louder, clearly urgent |
| Help | Help (English word) | Calm, slightly worried | Louder, clearly urgent |

### Section 2: Stern / Aggressive Lines — 6 clips

Read each line once. Sound firm and cold. Lines marked High should be said louder and
faster.

| Line (say it) | Meaning | How loud |
|---|---|---|
| Chup ho jao, warna theek nahi hoga | Be quiet, or it will not go well | Medium |
| Tumhe pata nahi main kya kar sakta hoon | You do not know what I can do | High |
| Shor mat karo | Do not make noise | Medium |
| Ab bhaagne ki koshish mat karna | Do not try to run now | High |
| Jo kaha hai wohi karo | Just do what I said | Medium |
| Yeh aakhri baar keh raha hoon | I am saying this for the last time | High |

### Section 3: Everyday Talking — 6 clips

Ask the question. Let the volunteer answer in their own words for about 5 to 7 seconds.
Do not make them read a script for this part.

| Topic | Ask this question |
|---|---|
| Daily routine | Aap ka aaj ka din kaisa guzra? |
| University | Aap ki class kis waqt hai? |
| Food | Aap ko khaane mein kya pasand hai? |
| Weather | Aaj mausam kaisa hai? |
| Travel | Aap university kaise aate hain? |
| Hobbies | Fursat mein aap kya karna pasand karte hain? |

## Saving Every File

**File name pattern:**

```
SHEA_VolunteerID_Class_Word_Take_Environment
```

- **Class letters:** `D` = Distress, `A` = Aggression, `N` = Everyday talking
- **Environment codes:**

| Code | Environment | Code | Environment |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Quiet room | E4 | Busy outdoors |
| E2 | Canteen | E5 | Corridor |
| E3 | Quiet outdoors | E6 | Car |

**Examples:**
- Female volunteer: `SHEA_F001_D_BCH_R01_E01.wav`
- Male volunteer: `SHEA_M001_D_HLP_R02_E03.wav`

> Note: the two worked examples above use 3-digit zero-padded volunteer IDs
> (F001, M001) even though the ID ranges elsewhere on this card are written as
> F01–F75 and M01–M20. Treat 3-digit zero-padding as the filename convention.

## After Recording

1. Save all files and back them up the same day.
2. Fill in the recording sheet: volunteer ID, gender, age group, region, environment(s),
   and which clips were recorded.
3. Thank the volunteer and remind them they can ask to have their data removed at any
   time by giving their volunteer ID.

Every clip you collect brings SheAlert one step closer to keeping someone safe. Thank you.
